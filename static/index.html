Asteroids

About this view

  • Distance is logarithmic. An asteroid's distance from Earth's surface is drawn as 0.5 × log10(1 + miss km / 6371) globe radii, so near and far passes both fit on screen. Distances are not proportional. The exact numbers are in the list and the detail view.
  • Directions are made up. NASA's feed gives how close an asteroid passes, not where in the sky. Each asteroid's direction is derived from a hash of its ID, so it is stable but not real.
  • The trails are imaginary. They are straight lines ending at the asteroid, sideways to Earth. Their length is proportional to the asteroid's speed. They are not real orbits or flight paths. Cyan trails are normal asteroids, orange-red ones are potentially hazardous.
  • Sizes are not to scale. Asteroids are drawn far larger than their true size relative to Earth (they would be invisible otherwise), and larger asteroids are only somewhat larger on screen.
  • Shapes are generated. Each asteroid's lumpy, cratered shape is procedural, derived from its ID. Only its diameter range is real data. The surface textures are of the Moon, not of the asteroids.
  • “Potentially hazardous” is NASA's label for asteroids that can come within 0.05 AU (about 7.5 million km) of Earth's orbit and are larger than about 140 m. It does not mean a collision is expected.
  • Units. 1 lunar distance = 384,400 km. 1 AU = about 149.6 million km. Miss distance is the closest approach to Earth's centre. The date range filters by closest-approach date.
  • Data from NASA NeoWs / JPL. Textures: NASA SVS (LROC Moon colour map), three.js and three-globe example images.
